Generate from dice rolls (advanced)

Roll a standard six-sided die and tap each result below. This derives your phrase from physical randomness instead of your device's random number generator — useful if you'd rather not have to trust that generator at all. The word count chosen above sets how many rolls are needed.

0 / 100 rolls

Rolls are joined with commas and hashed with SHA-256, truncated to the entropy length needed — you can redo that calculation with any tool to check it independently. No device randomness is mixed in.

Add a passphrase (optional, advanced)

BIP-39 calls this the 25th word. Combined with your phrase, it derives a completely different wallet. There is no way to recover a lost passphrase — leave it blank unless you specifically want this.

"Generate" below draws random words from the same BIP-39 list as your phrase above. That's fine cryptographically — PBKDF2 treats a passphrase as arbitrary text either way, and the two are drawn independently — the only reason dedicated tools use a separate word list is so the two don't look alike on paper.

Good practice
  • Write the words by hand, on paper or stamped into steel — don't photograph, screenshot, or type them anywhere else.
  • Generate on a device that has never been and will never be online, if the wallet will hold real value.
  • Test recovery with a small amount before trusting a wallet with more.
  • For spending, consider a dedicated offline signer such as SeedSigner rather than a general-purpose computer.
  • The online/offline badge above reflects your OS network status, not true internet reachability — when it matters, disconnect physically rather than trust the badge alone.

Entropy comes from crypto.getRandomValues, encoded as a BIP-39 mnemonic with a SHA-256 checksum, and the seed below is derived with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 at 2048 rounds — the same steps every BIP-39 wallet runs. This page makes no network requests, and its Content-Security-Policy blocks outbound connections at the browser level even if the code were somehow tampered with.

QR codes use the Standard SeedQR format and the qrcode-generator library by Kazuhiko Arase (MIT) — embedded in full below, no external request.
